Solarpunk, in practice

This is a short course, not a school. Four lessons. You can read them in one sitting. There is no login and no certificate. The point is to leave with a way of building you can keep.

1. What solarpunk is

Solarpunk is a picture of a future people can keep. Sunlight does heavy work. Tools are made to be repaired. Neighborhoods share gardens, energy, and what they make. It is for everyone, not a closed club. The delight is the point: a future you can want, and then build a small piece of.

2. A tool you can pause

Before a workflow carries real work, it needs a memory of what it did, a fallback when a step fails, and a review where a person can stop it. An agent can sort and draft. It waits. If you are not there, the work sits. That is the design.

3. A sensor that stays nearby

Soil, rain, and solar do not need a distant account to keep working. A small message on the local network is enough. The person who maintains the sensor should be able to read the status without a specialist.

4. How to keep it

Host it yourself when you can. Write down how to open it. Name the person who can pause it. A system you cannot repair is a system you do not really have.
